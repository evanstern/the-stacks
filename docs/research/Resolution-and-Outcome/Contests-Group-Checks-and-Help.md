# Contests, Group Checks and Help

_How sources resolve opposed efforts, several characters working at one task, and one character assisting another. Neutral and descriptive._

## SRD 5.2.1 (CC-BY-4.0)

- Help has two modes. Assist an ability check: pick one of your skill or tool proficiencies and one ally near enough to assist verbally or physically; the ally has Advantage on their next check with that skill or tool, and the benefit expires at the start of your next turn. Assist an attack roll: distract an enemy within 5 feet to give an ally Advantage on the next attack against that enemy, expiring at the start of your next turn. The GM has final say on whether the assistance is possible. (SRD p.10, p.182-183, Help action)
- Help competes with other actions: a character cannot use Search to look for traps and Help someone opening a stuck door in the same action. (SRD p.10, One Thing at a Time)
- Help is also the action used to stabilize a creature at 0 HP, with a DC 10 Wisdom (Medicine) check. (SRD p.18, Stabilizing a Character)
- In social interaction the group should lead with whoever has the relevant skill; the example sends the Deception-proficient Rogue to trick a guard. (SRD p.11, Social Interaction > Ability Checks)
- Escape from a grapple is the player's Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics) check against the grappler's escape DC (8 + Strength modifier + proficiency bonus for an Unarmed Strike grapple). The target of a grapple or shove chooses whether to save with Strength or Dexterity. (SRD p.182, Grappling; p.190, Unarmed Strike)
- Each participant rolls a Dexterity check for Initiative; for a group of identical monsters the GM rolls once. The SRD text names no group-check rule for non-combat tasks; the group-check pattern is not in the sections read. (SRD p.13, Initiative)

## Books

- DMG 2014 (Ch 8 > Ability Checks > Contests): a contest compares two ability checks against each other instead of a DC. It is used when an effort is directly opposed by a creature's own effort. The DM picks the abilities: the same ability for arm wrestling or holding a door shut, different abilities for hiding (Dexterity against Wisdom).
- DMG 2014 (Ch 8 > Social Interaction > 3. Charisma Check > Aiding the Check): any character who took part in the conversation may roll. Others who contribute something positive give advantage; a gaffe by a helper imposes disadvantage. Multiple goals in one interaction can need more than one check.
- DMG 2014 (Ch 8 > Exploration > Noticing Other Creatures): if neither side is stealthy, creatures notice each other automatically within sight or hearing range. If a side hides, its Stealth results are compared to the other group's passive Perception scores.
- DMG 2014 (Ch 8 > Combat > Using and Tracking Conditions): conditions applied on the fly should be intuitive (a sleeping character is unconscious; a stumbling one is prone).
- DMG 2014 (Ch 9 > Combat Options > Action Options): contest-based options for physical interaction: climbing onto a bigger creature (Athletics or Acrobatics contested by the target's Acrobatics), disarm (attack roll contested by Athletics or Acrobatics), overrun and tumble (contested checks to pass through a space), shove aside, mark. The attacker's size and grip set advantage or disadvantage.
- DMG 2014 (Ch 9 > Adventuring Options > Plot Points): an optional shared-authority tool. A player spends a point to add a fact the table must accept, or the player on their right adds a complication, or the player becomes the DM. This transfers some narrative authority from DM to players.
- XGtE (Ch 2 > Simultaneous Effects): when two effects occur at once on a creature's turn, the person controlling that creature (player or DM) picks the order.
- TCoE (DM's Tools > Parleying with Monsters): offering something the monster wants grants advantage on communication checks, a form of cooperative leverage the party can set up.

## Web

Sly Flourish

- Handling Group Skill Checks (https://slyflourish.com/group_skill_checks.html): describes the 5e PHB options: Help gives the helped character advantage, and a group check succeeds if half the group passes. It adds a faster option: the most skilled character rolls with advantage while others help. It suggests skipping the roll if one success is enough for the whole group, and using a full group check when the table wants to see individuals succeed.
- Our Ability Check Toolbox (https://slyflourish.com/ability_check_toolbox.html): splits tasks by who can reasonably attempt them. Single-actor tasks (disarm a trap, pick a lock) allow only the first attempt; multi-actor tasks (searching a room, scouting) use a group check where more than half must succeed. For Aid Another it asks the helper to describe the help; for Guidance it asks the caster to describe the bonus in the fiction.
- Rolling Lots of D20s? Assume One Quarter Succeeds (https://slyflourish.com/one_quarter_succeed.html): for many simultaneous checks, saves or attacks, assume about one quarter succeed (half with advantage, about one in ten with disadvantage), optionally adjusting for variance. The article argues this shortens dice time and suits groups of monsters.
- Roll to Target (https://slyflourish.com/roll_to_target.html): a GM roll made openly in view of the table to choose which character a monster attacks. The article reports it reduces the sense of being singled out.

The Alexandrian

- Random GM Tip: Dogpiling the Check (https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/49184/roleplaying-games/random-gm-tip-dogpiling-the-check): names three problems with several characters retrying the same check: metagaming, pacing and balance (a 70 percent check becomes about 96 percent with four extra 50 percent rollers). It distinguishes dogpiling from simultaneous cooperative group actions.

Angry GM

- Help! My Players are Talking to Things! (https://theangrygm.com/help-my-players-are-talking-to-things/): barter, if used, should be a single opposed check; bribes are accepted when plausible, with a roll allowed but not required. The article warns players when an offer is wildly excessive.

## Where sources disagree

- Group check rule. Sly Flourish describes the PHB group check as succeeding when half the group passes, and elsewhere as more than half (https://slyflourish.com/group_skill_checks.html, https://slyflourish.com/ability_check_toolbox.html). The Alexandrian's gradated group check also uses half (https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/49184/roleplaying-games/random-gm-tip-dogpiling-the-check). No SRD 5.2.1 group-check text was found in the sections read.
- Shared checks versus single roller. Sly Flourish offers a faster option (best character rolls with advantage while others help) and a full group check to spotlight individuals (https://slyflourish.com/group_skill_checks.html). The Alexandrian treats several characters retrying one check as a problem (metagaming, pacing, balance) (https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/49184/roleplaying-games/random-gm-tip-dogpiling-the-check).
- Edition divergence: Help. SRD p.182-183 requires choosing a skill or tool proficiency and gives the ally Advantage on the next check with it, expiring at the start of the helper's next turn. DMG 2014 Ch 8 describes helping only inside the social check (advantage to the roller, disadvantage for a bad contribution). The 2014 Player's Handbook Help action was not compared.
- Edition divergence: Hide and noticing. SRD p.183 sets a flat DC 15 and uses the total as the DC to find the hider; DMG 2014 compares Stealth against passive Perception.
- Book versus SRD on opposed efforts: DMG 2014 Contests compare two checks directly. In the SRD 5.2.1 sections read, opposed efforts use a DC derived from the opponent (the grapple escape DC; the Hide total as a DC); a named contest procedure was not found in those sections.
