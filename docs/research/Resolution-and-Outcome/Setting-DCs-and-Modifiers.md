# Setting DCs and Modifiers

_How sources say difficulty numbers are chosen and how bonuses, penalties, Advantage and Disadvantage are built up. Neutral and descriptive._

## SRD 5.2.1 (CC-BY-4.0)

- The rules supply DCs for some checks, but "the GM ultimately sets them" (SRD 5.2.1, CC-BY-4.0). Typical Difficulty Classes: Very easy 5, Easy 10, Medium 15, Hard 20, Very hard 25, Nearly impossible 30. (SRD p.6, Difficulty Class)
- Total = d20 + ability modifier + proficiency bonus if relevant + circumstantial bonuses or penalties. The GM states the target number and tells players whether the roll succeeds. The target for checks and saves is a DC; for attacks it is Armor Class. (SRD p.6, D20 Tests)
- Ability modifier runs from -5 (score 1) to +10 (score 30); score 10-11 is +0 and 20-21 is +5. (SRD p.5-6, Ability Scores and Modifiers)
- Proficiency bonus is +2 through level 4 (or CR 4), +3 at 5-8, +4 at 9-12, +5 at 13-16, +6 at 17-20. It cannot be added more than once to the same roll. (SRD p.8, Proficiency Bonus)
- The GM has the ultimate say on whether a skill is relevant to a check. A character without the proficiency still rolls, without the bonus. (SRD p.8, Skill Proficiencies)
- Tool proficiency adds the proficiency bonus to checks that use the tool. If the character also has the matching skill, the check gains Advantage. (SRD p.9, Equipment Proficiencies > Tools)
- Roll two d20 and take the higher (Advantage) or lower (Disadvantage). Multiple sources do not stack. Any Advantage plus any Disadvantage cancel to a single d20, even if the counts are unequal. The GM "can also decide that circumstances grant Advantage or impose Disadvantage" (SRD 5.2.1, CC-BY-4.0). (SRD p.7-8, Advantage/Disadvantage)
- Passive score: 10 + the Perception bonus, +5 with Advantage, -5 with Disadvantage. (SRD p.186, Glossary: Passive Perception)
- Travel pace sets modifiers without a DC: Fast imposes Disadvantage on Perception, Survival and Stealth; Normal imposes Disadvantage on Stealth; Slow grants Advantage on Perception and Survival. (SRD p.12, Travel Pace)
- The Travel Terrain table gives terrain-keyed DCs for foraging (10-20), navigation (5-15) and search (10-20). (SRD p.192, Gameplay Toolbox)
- Hide: a DC 15 Dexterity (Stealth) check, only while Heavily Obscured or behind three-quarters or total cover and out of enemy line of sight. The total becomes the DC for others to find the character with Perception. (SRD p.183, Glossary: Hide)
- Influence: the default DC is 15 or the monster's Intelligence score, whichever is higher. Friendly attitude gives Advantage (p.182), Hostile gives Disadvantage (p.183), Indifferent is the default (p.184). (SRD p.184, Glossary: Influence)
- Vision and light: Lightly Obscured gives Disadvantage on sight-based Perception; Heavily Obscured means the character is Blinded for seeing purposes. (SRD p.11-12, Vision and Light)
- Cover: half cover +2 AC and Dex saves; three-quarters cover +5; total cover blocks direct targeting. The GM determines cover and attack Advantage or Disadvantage as step 2 of an attack. (SRD p.15, Cover)
- Climbing slippery or handhold-poor surfaces, "at the GM's option" (SRD 5.2.1, CC-BY-4.0), needs a DC 15 Strength (Athletics) check. Long jump and swimming entries give similar optional DC 10 and DC 15 checks. The pattern is a named default DC with the call left to the GM. (SRD p.179, p.185, p.189, Glossary)

## Books

- DMG 2014 (Ch 8 > Using Ability Scores > Difficulty Class): when no rule or adventure gives a DC, the DM judges how hard the task is and picks from the Typical DCs table (5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30). The numbers are meant to be memorized.
- DMG 2014 (Ch 8 > Difficulty Class > Typical DCs): a DC 5 task normally succeeds without a roll; using only DC 10, 15 and 20 works fine; a character with a score of 10 and no proficiency succeeds an Easy task about half the time; higher DCs must be judged against character level (DC 25 is very hard at low level and reasonable after about 10th level; DC 30 needs a natural 19 or 20 even from a level 20 character with a 20 in the stat and proficiency).
- DMG 2014 (Ch 8 > Difficulty Class > Variant: Automatic Success): optional. A check auto-succeeds when its DC is at or below the ability score minus 5. Proficiency in the skill or tool auto-succeeds DC 10 or lower (DC 15 or lower at 11th level and up). The stated downside is predictability: players always send the highest-score character, and raising DCs to restore risk makes outcomes luck-driven.
- DMG 2014 (Ch 8 > Using Ability Scores > Proficiency): when a player asks, the DM can apply a skill to a check if the player justifies it; the example is a long swim treated as a Constitution (Athletics) check. The test for assigning proficiency: could training and practice improve the task?
- DMG 2014 (Ch 8 > Advantage and Disadvantage): grant advantage for circumstances outside the creature's own capability, a helpful environment, exceptional creativity or cunning in the player's description, or earlier actions that improve odds. Mirror cases give disadvantage, including an element of the plan or description that makes success less likely. Because advantage and disadvantage cancel, the DM does not count the factors on each side (worked example: a running wizard versus a noisy ogre ambush).
- DMG 2014 (Ch 8 > Exploration > Tracking > Tracking DCs): by surface, soft such as snow 10, dirt or grass 15, bare stone 20; +5 per day since passage; -5 if the creature left a trail such as blood. Obvious tracks need no roll; with no trail at all, tracking can be ruled impossible.
- DMG 2014 (Ch 8 > Social Interaction > Resolving Interactions > 3. Charisma Check): the creature's current attitude and the size of the risk or sacrifice asked of it set the DC; each step less friendly raises the DC. Helpers who contribute meaningfully give the roller advantage; a counterproductive remark gives disadvantage.
- DMG 2014 (Ch 9 DM's Workshop > Ability Options > Hero Points): optional. 5 hero points at 1st level, then 5 + half level per level. A point spent after the roll adds a d6 to the d20 (one per roll) and converts a failed death save into a success.
- DMG 2014 (Ch 9 > Ability Options > Proficiency Dice and Skill Variants): optional. A proficiency die replaces the proficiency bonus (more variance). Another variant removes skills and gives each character two proficient abilities. Both change how modifiers are built before the DM sets a DC.
- TCoE (DM's Tools > Parleying with Monsters > Monster Research): researching what a creature wants uses a skill chosen by creature type (Arcana for Aberrations, History for Giants and Humanoids). The DC is 10 + the creature's Challenge Rating.
- XGtE (Ch 2 > Spellcasting > Identifying a Spell): the DC to identify a spell is 15 + the spell's level, with advantage if the identifier belongs to the class that cast it.
- XGtE (Ch 2 > Sleep > Waking Someone): passive Wisdom (Perception) acts as a threshold with no roll. Whispers wake a sleeper only at passive 20 or higher within 10 feet; normal speech wakes a sleeper at passive 15 or higher in an otherwise silent place.

## Web

- Our Ability Check Toolbox (https://slyflourish.com/ability_check_toolbox.html): equates strong roleplay or a good approach to roughly advantage, a +3 to +5 bonus depending on difficulty, or a lowered DC. It allows advantage for a trait or background that gives a real edge (a sailor examining a wrecked ship), not for abilities already built into a class feature. It states that it gives no DC table.
- The Art of Rulings (https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/4238/roleplaying-games/the-art-of-rulings): worked examples against a fixed DC 17 Search. A generic search gets the flat check, probing the right spot gets a bonus (around +10 in the pit-trap example), and a decisive method (smashing the chest bottom) succeeds automatically. Searching the wrong place or for the wrong thing can impose a penalty or guarantee failure.
- Art of Rulings 12: Hidden vs. Open Difficulty Numbers (https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/39142/roleplaying-games/art-of-rulings-12-hidden-vs-open-difficulty-numbers): reveal the target number when characters could observe what determines difficulty (visible chasm width); hide it when they could not (an unseen tribesman's camouflage). It offers an optional estimate based on what the character knows. It names hiding as the default when unsure and notes some systems reverse that default for practical reasons.
- 5 Simple Rules for Dating My Teenaged Skill System (https://theangrygm.com/five-simple-rules-for-dating-my-teenaged-skill-system/): the author sets difficulties at the table with brief reminders such as difficult or unlikely and gives no numeric table. For knowledge, passive checks are preferred: knowledge at or below the character's passive score (10 plus modifier) is known.
- Resolving Social Actions (https://theangrygm.com/resolving-social-actions/): a five-element NPC social stat block (Source of Conflict, Disposition, Patience, Motivation, Mood). Difficulty starts from Disposition, then adjusts by action type (Convince hardest, Connect easiest) and by how well the approach fits the NPC's Mood and Motivations.
- Running Roleplay and Intrigue Adventures (https://slyflourish.com/running_intrigue_adventures.html): a high-Charisma character's score can serve as a negotiation baseline when the character is more skilled than the player. The reverse holds too: a charismatic player's approach can be the baseline even when the character is low in Charisma, without always calling for a roll.
- Declaring Your Players' Social Actions (https://theangrygm.com/declaring-social-actions/): the article treats modifiers and results as coming from the game's ability and skill checks, and states that dice are not fudged.
- Help! My Players are Talking to Things! (https://theangrygm.com/help-my-players-are-talking-to-things/): the article discourages bonuses for acting ability because it is a talent not all players share.

## Where sources disagree

- Bonus for player acting or roleplay. Angry GM discourages bonuses for acting ability (https://theangrygm.com/help-my-players-are-talking-to-things/). Sly Flourish grants advantage or +3 to +5 for strong roleplay (https://slyflourish.com/ability_check_toolbox.html). DMG 2014 (Ch 8 > Advantage and Disadvantage) lists exceptional creativity or cunning in the player's description as a ground for advantage.
- Hidden versus open DCs. The Alexandrian hides numbers the characters could not observe and reveals those they could (https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/39142/roleplaying-games/art-of-rulings-12-hidden-vs-open-difficulty-numbers). Angry GM advises warning players of foreseeable risks and costs before they commit (https://theangrygm.com/declare-determine-describe/). The Alexandrian's comments record the author defending hiding a roll's purpose to preserve character perspective, against a commenter who wanted purposes stated. SRD 5.2.1 (p.6) has the GM state the target number and tell players whether the roll succeeds.
- Numeric DC tables versus table-side labels. SRD p.6 and DMG 2014 give a numeric ladder (5 to 30). Angry GM sets difficulty with short labels and no table (https://theangrygm.com/five-simple-rules-for-dating-my-teenaged-skill-system/). Sly Flourish states it gives no DC table (https://slyflourish.com/ability_check_toolbox.html).
- Edition divergence: the label for DC 15 is Moderate in DMG 2014 and Medium in SRD p.6. The numeric ladder is identical.
- Edition divergence: Hide. SRD p.183 states a flat DC 15 Stealth check and uses the total as the DC for Perception to find the hider. DMG 2014 (Ch 8 > Exploration > Noticing Other Creatures) compares Stealth against passive Perception.
- Edition divergence: Help. SRD p.182-183 requires choosing a skill or tool proficiency and gives the ally Advantage on the next check with it, expiring at the start of the helper's next turn. DMG 2014 Ch 8 describes helping only inside the social check. The 2014 Player's Handbook Help action was not compared.
- Edition divergence: Inspiration. SRD p.8 and p.183 (Heroic Inspiration) is spent to reroll any die after rolling it, and the new roll must be used. DMG 2014 (Ch 8 > Inspiration) grants advantage on one roll, with a variant that allows spending it after the d20. Only one at a time in both.
- Book versus SRD: the SRD names default DCs for specific actions (Hide 15, climbing 15, Influence 15 or Intelligence). The DMG 2014 table is a general ladder with tips on level scaling and an optional auto-success rule; SRD 5.2.1 Playing the Game has no equivalent of the level-scaling tips.
