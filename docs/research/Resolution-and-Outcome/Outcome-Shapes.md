# Outcome Shapes

_The forms a resolved roll can take beyond pass and fail: costs on success, degrees of failure, natural 20 and natural 1, critical hits, and the DM's own shaping of results. Neutral and descriptive._

## SRD 5.2.1 (CC-BY-4.0)

- Binary baseline: a total equal to or above the target succeeds; below fails. (SRD p.6, D20 Tests)
- A natural 20 on an attack roll hits regardless of modifiers or AC and is a Critical Hit; a natural 1 on an attack roll misses regardless. The rule is stated only for attack rolls. (SRD p.7, Attack Rolls > Rolling 20 or 1)
- A Critical Hit rolls the attack's damage dice twice and adds modifiers once. Extra dice such as Sneak Attack are also rolled twice. (SRD p.16, Critical Hits)
- Death saving throw: roll 1d20; 10 or higher succeeds. A natural 1 counts as two failures; a natural 20 regains 1 Hit Point. Three successes stabilize and three failures kill. (SRD p.17-18, Death Saving Throws)
- Damage to a character at 0 HP is a death-save failure; a Critical Hit causes two. Damage at or above the HP maximum kills. (SRD p.18, Damage at 0 Hit Points)
- Saves that deal damage commonly deal half on success, so a successful save can still carry a cost. (SRD p.16, Half Damage)
- A failed Influence check does not fail the NPC forever; it locks the same approach for 24 hours or a GM-set duration. (SRD p.184, Influence)
- A save's result "is detailed in the effect that caused it" (SRD 5.2.1, CC-BY-4.0): the effect owns the consequence. (SRD p.7, Saving Throws)
- Bloodied: a creature at half HP or fewer is Bloodied, which has no game effect on its own but can trigger other effects. (SRD p.17, Bloodied)
- The GM chooses whether to use the average or the rolled value of a monster's damage. (SRD p.189, Damage Notation)

## Books

- DMG 2014 (Ch 8 > Using Ability Scores > Resolution and Consequences): the DM determines consequences of every attack, check and save. Defaults are simple (hit deals damage; failed save causes the effect; check meeting the DC succeeds), and the section offers flourishes to avoid pure black and white.
- DMG 2014 (Ch 8 > Resolution and Consequences > Success at a Cost): when a roll fails by only 1 or 2, the DM may let it succeed with a complication. The section's samples span attacks, saves, social checks and climbing; each pairs the success with a new problem in the scene. Complications are setbacks that change the situation, not penalties. The same tool can apply when a roll hits the DC exactly.
- DMG 2014 (Ch 8 > Resolution and Consequences > Degrees of Failure): failure can be tiered by margin. A small miss withholds the goal; a miss by 5 or more adds a worse consequence. The section illustrates this with a trap and a social request.
- DMG 2014 (Ch 8 > Resolution and Consequences > Critical Success or Failure): a 20 or 1 on a check or save has no special effect by default; the DM may use it to scale the result, such as a broken tool on a failed lockpick 1 or an extra clue on an Investigation 20.
- DMG 2014 (Ch 8 > Social Interaction > Determining Characteristics): an Insight check failing by 10 or more may return a false or inverted characteristic, a tiered failure that corrupts information instead of withholding it.
- DMG 2014 (Ch 8 > Table Rules > Dice Rolling): behind a screen the DM may soften a second consecutive critical hit that would kill a character, with the warning not to do this often and not to let on, or players may conclude there is no real risk or that the DM plays favorites.
- DMG 2014 (Ch 8 > Combat > Monsters and Critical Hits): a monster crits by the same rule as a PC. When the DM uses average damage, the crit adds a roll of the damage dice on top.
- DMG 2014 (Ch 8 > Combat > Handling Mobs): replaces many attack rolls with a lookup. The DM subtracts the attack bonus from the target's AC to get the d20 number needed and reads off how many attackers are needed for one hit (1 attacker at 1-5, 2 at 6-12, 3 at 13-14, 4 at 15-16, 5 at 17-18, 10 at 19, 20 at 20). The system ignores critical hits; the DM returns to individual rolls as numbers dwindle.
- DMG 2014 (Ch 8 > Combat > Improvising Damage): a damage ladder from 1d10 to 24d10 with example hazards, and a Setback/Dangerous/Deadly table keyed to character level (levels 1-4: 1d10, 2d10, 4d10).
- DMG 2014 (Ch 9 > Combat Options > Injuries): optional lingering injuries on a critical hit, dropping to 0 HP without dying, or failing a death save by 5 or more. A variant converts the injury into a roleplayed flaw.
- DMG 2014 (Ch 9 > Combat Options > Massive Damage): optional. Damage from one source at or above half HP maximum forces a DC 15 Constitution save or a roll on a System Shock table (outcomes range from dropping to 0 HP to losing reactions until the next turn).
- DMG 2014 (Ch 9 > Combat Options > Hitting Cover): optional. A missed ranged attack that would have hit without cover strikes the cover object or covering creature.
- DMG 2014 (Ch 9 > Combat Options > Cleaving through Creatures): optional. Excess damage from a melee kill of an undamaged creature carries to another creature in reach if the attack roll would hit it.
- DMG 2014 (Ch 8 > Inspiration): inspiration lets a player gain advantage on one roll; the Heroism variant allows spending it after the d20 is rolled, acting as a cushion against failure.

## Web

- Our Ability Check Toolbox (https://slyflourish.com/ability_check_toolbox.html): recommends shades-of-gray results that turn a poor roll into a complicated but interesting outcome. It presents no degrees-of-success table or tier system.
- Let Characters Automatically Succeed Sometimes (https://slyflourish.com/let_characters_auto_succeed.html): succeed but roll anyway. When success is granted the character still rolls to see how well it goes; a high result reveals more or yields a better outcome, a low result means a clumsier success. The d20 is treated as a gradient.
- Handling Group Skill Checks (https://slyflourish.com/group_skill_checks.html): success and failure need not be binary. High rolls can reveal extra information or exceed expectations; very low rolls can create complications worse than plain failure. Outcomes can be narrative or mechanical.
- Running Roleplay and Intrigue Adventures (https://slyflourish.com/running_intrigue_adventures.html): treat a roll as a matter of degree and, when a bad roll would end a line of inquiry, let the story continue along a harder but still interesting path.
- The Tao of the Dice (https://theangrygm.com/tao-of-dice/): dice produce a simple yes or no, with emphatic versions of each. The article advises knowing all possible outcomes and their consequences before rolling.
- Adjudicate Actions Like a Boss (https://theangrygm.com/adjudicate-actions-like-a-boss/): separates Outcome (the direct result of intention and success or failure, possibly partial progress) from Consequences (world changes that follow from the chosen approach regardless of the roll). Partial progress is used sparingly, and only when the party can perceive it or when dragging the action out serves the game.
- Help! My Players are Talking to Things! (https://theangrygm.com/help-my-players-are-talking-to-things/): a successful social action may fully succeed or only remove part of an NPC's Objection, at the GM's decision. Consequences should exist on success or failure and should be perceivable by players.
- Failure for the Beginning GM (https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/46186/roleplaying-games/failure-for-the-beginning-gm): failing forward is a failed check that still yields success in the fiction with a complication. Default complications: extra cost, damage, lost or broken equipment, extra time, being discovered.
- No source in the web set treats critical hits or natural 1 and 20 on ability checks as a distinct outcome shape. The 5e crit rule is not covered by these articles.

## Where sources disagree

- Narrating partial progress. Angry GM treats partial progress as an outcome to use sparingly (https://theangrygm.com/adjudicate-actions-like-a-boss/). Sly Flourish and The Alexandrian treat graded or complicated results as a routine tool (https://slyflourish.com/group_skill_checks.html, https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/46186/roleplaying-games/failure-for-the-beginning-gm).
- Book versus web on complications: DMG 2014 limits Success at a Cost to a near miss of 1 or 2 (or an exact hit); Sly Flourish applies shades of gray to poor rolls generally without a stated margin; The Alexandrian's quick-and-dirty method applies to any failed check and falls back to granting success or no retries when no complication comes to mind.
- Book versus web on degrees: DMG 2014 gives margin-keyed tiers (failure by 5 or more, by 10 or more). The web set presents no tier table; Sly Flourish names degrees without margins.
- Book versus web on high rolls: DMG 2014 (Critical Success or Failure) offers a scaled result on a 20 as an option; Sly Flourish's succeed-but-roll-anyway applies the whole d20 range to quality of success.
- Whether the result stands. Angry GM says the GM should let a rolled result stand and not fudge (https://theangrygm.com/tao-of-dice/, https://theangrygm.com/declaring-social-actions/). The Alexandrian's Failure for the Beginning GM likewise says results should stand. DMG 2014 (Table Rules > Dice Rolling) describes softening a lethal second crit behind a screen, with caveats. Sly Flourish has an article on when and how to cheat that this pass did not fetch.
- Edition divergence: natural 20 and 1. SRD p.7 states the automatic hit and miss only for attack rolls. DMG 2014 says the same by default for checks and saves (no special effect) and invites the DM to scale outcomes on a 1 or 20.
- Edition divergence: Bloodied. SRD p.17 defines it as half HP or fewer with no game effect on its own. DMG 2014 treats it as a descriptive cue the DM may use for a monster at half.
- Edition divergence: fail-forward tools. Success at a Cost, Degrees of Failure and ten-times-time success live only in DMG 2014 Ch 8. SRD 5.2.1 Playing the Game has no equivalent adjudication advice.
- Edition divergence: monster death. SRD p.18 says a monster dies at 0 HP unless the GM chooses to treat it like a character. No parallel text was found in the DMG sections read.
- Edition divergence: knock out. SRD p.17 and p.184 let a melee attacker reduce a target to 1 HP and Unconscious instead of killing; the option ends after a short rest, healing or a DC 10 Medicine check. It was not found in the DMG sections read.
