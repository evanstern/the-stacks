# Exploration, Hazards, Traps and Puzzles

_How the sources treat moving through dungeons, wilderness and settlements, environmental hazards, traps, and puzzles. Neutral and descriptive: this note lists what the gathered sources cover. It states no verdicts._

## Tags in this area

- `exploration` — dungeon, wilderness and settlement movement
- `hazards-traps` — environmental hazards and traps
- `puzzles` — puzzles and riddles

Tags were assigned by the gather passes. One entry can carry several tags and can appear in several craft notes.

### Xanathar's Guide to Everything (XGtE, 2017)

- XGtE › Dungeon Master's Tools › Falling — Expands the falling rule with a per-round rate of descent so mid-air falls become something characters can react to. _[hazards-traps, adjudication, combat-running]_
- XGtE › Dungeon Master's Tools › Falling › Rate of Falling — Treats falling from great heights as taking time rather than being instantaneous. _[hazards-traps, time-tracking]_
- XGtE › Dungeon Master's Tools › Random Encounters › A World of Possibilities — Builds on the core random-encounter advice: treat each result as a potential challenge, not necessarily a fight, and decide how monsters will react. _[encounter-design, improvisation, exploration]_
- XGtE › Dungeon Master's Tools › Random Encounters › Encounter Tables by Terrain — Heading-level only. Tables for arctic, coastal, desert, forest, grassland, hill, mountain, swamp, Underdark, underwater, and urban terrain, each split by level band. _[encounter-design, exploration]_
- XGtE › Traps Revisited › Simple Traps — Frames a one-shot trap as an encounter with its own statistics, trigger, and counterplay. _[hazards-traps, encounter-design]_
- XGtE › Traps Revisited › Simple Traps › Elements of a Simple Trap — Names the parts of a trap description used for quick reference at the table. _[hazards-traps]_
- XGtE › Traps Revisited › Simple Traps › Running a Simple Trap — Teaches checking passive scores first and rewarding clever player investigation. _[hazards-traps, adjudication, player-agency]_
- XGtE › Traps Revisited › Example Simple Traps — Heading-level only. Nine sample traps across level bands. _[hazards-traps]_
- XGtE › Traps Revisited › Designing Simple Traps — Stepwise guidance: purpose, level and lethality, triggers, effects, disarming, and placement. _[hazards-traps, adventure-design]_
- XGtE › Traps Revisited › Complex Traps — Describes traps that keep acting over rounds like a legendary monster. _[hazards-traps, combat-running]_
- XGtE › Traps Revisited › Complex Traps › Running a Complex Trap — Treat the active elements as acting on fixed initiative counts each round. _[hazards-traps, combat-running]_
- XGtE › Traps Revisited › Complex Traps › Experience for Complex Traps — XP awards for overcoming complex traps and judging when they count as overcome. _[rewards, hazards-traps]_
- XGtE › Traps Revisited › Example Complex Traps — Heading-level only. Three sample complex traps. _[hazards-traps]_
- XGtE › Traps Revisited › Designing Complex Traps — Design guidance covering map, active, constant, and dynamic elements, triggers, initiative, and layered counterplay. _[hazards-traps, adventure-design]_

### Tasha's Cauldron of Everything (TCoE, 2020)

- TCoE › Ch 4 Dungeon Master's Tools › Environmental Hazards — Presents unusual regions, magical phenomena, and natural hazards for adventures. _[hazards-traps, world-building]_
- TCoE › Ch 4 Dungeon Master's Tools › Environmental Hazards › Supernatural Regions — Seven region types, such as haunted or infested, that change what a place does to those within it. _[hazards-traps, world-building, conditions-effects]_
- TCoE › Ch 4 Dungeon Master's Tools › Environmental Hazards › Magical Phenomena — Phenomena such as eldritch storms, enchanted springs, and primal fruit that add magical color. _[hazards-traps, description-narration]_
- TCoE › Ch 4 Dungeon Master's Tools › Environmental Hazards › Natural Hazards — Hazards like avalanches and falling into water or onto creatures, plus spells that stand in for nature's effects. _[hazards-traps, adjudication]_
- TCoE › Ch 5 Puzzles › Why Use Puzzles? — Argues puzzles let players use their wits and collaborate and shows when to add them. _[puzzles, adventure-design]_
- TCoE › Ch 5 Puzzles › Why Use Puzzles? › Puzzle Elements — Each puzzle supplies read-aloud text, features, a solution, hint checks, and customization advice. _[puzzles, description-narration]_
- TCoE › Ch 5 Puzzles › Why Use Puzzles? › Hints — Offers hint checks as skill-and-DC pairs to help stuck players without handing over answers. _[puzzles, ability-checks, dc-setting]_
- TCoE › Ch 5 Puzzles › Why Use Puzzles? › Running Puzzles — Teaches presenting a puzzle, answering clarifying questions, and not giving away the solution. _[puzzles, secrets-information, improvisation]_
- TCoE › Ch 5 Puzzles › Sample Puzzles — Heading-level only. Thirteen puzzles graded easy, medium, or hard (for example paintings, floor tiles, key and lock, gems, maze riddles, skull grid, dials, components, password, coins, elements, hallway, menu), each with handouts and customization notes. _[puzzles]_
- TCoE › Ch 5 Puzzles › Sample Puzzles › Customizing the Puzzle — Recurring subsection on reskinning a puzzle for a different setting or difficulty. _[puzzles, improvisation]_
