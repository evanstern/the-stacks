# NPCs and Social Interaction

_How the sources treat non-player characters (design, portrayal, villains, followers) and the resolution of social encounters. Neutral and descriptive: this note lists what the gathered sources cover. It states no verdicts._

## Tags in this area

- `npcs` — non-player characters, villains, followers
- `social-interaction` — social encounters and their resolution

Tags were assigned by the gather passes. One entry can carry several tags and can appear in several craft notes.

## Entries by source

### Dungeon Master's Guide (DMG 2014, 5th Edition 2014)

- DMG 2014 › Ch 3 Creating Adventures › Adventure Types › Location-Based Adventures — Step-by-step recipe: set party goals, pick key NPCs, detail the place, craft an introduction and a climax, then plan encounters; supported by roll tables for goals, villains, allies, patrons. _[adventure-design, npcs, encounter-design]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 3 Creating Adventures › Adventure Types › Event-Based Adventures — Recipe built around a villain's timeline: choose the villain, script their actions, set party goals, anticipate the villain's reactions to interference, and detail only the places that matter. _[adventure-design, npcs, player-agency]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 3 Creating Adventures › Adventure Types › Intrigue — Explains building a power-struggle adventure with several villains and tracking the party's influence with factions or individuals. _[adventure-design, social-interaction, npcs]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › Designing NPCs — Argues NPCs need less complexity than player characters and teaches deciding how much detail each deserves. _[npcs]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › Designing NPCs › Quick NPCs — Teaches giving incidental NPCs one or two memorable traits and stats only when they pose a threat. _[npcs, improvisation]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › Designing NPCs › Detailed NPCs — Teaches summarizing a major NPC in about ten sentences covering occupation, look, ability, talent, mannerism, interaction, knowledge, ideal, bond, and flaw or secret. _[npcs]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › Designing NPCs › Detailed NPCs › Occupation and History — One-line past and job that hints at who the NPC was. _[npcs]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › Designing NPCs › Detailed NPCs › Appearance — One distinctive visual feature, with a table for inspiration. _[npcs, description-narration]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › Designing NPCs › Detailed NPCs › Abilities — Note only standout strengths or weaknesses and use them to inform portrayal instead of rolling scores. _[npcs]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › Designing NPCs › Detailed NPCs › Talent — One special skill the NPC has, with a table. _[npcs]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › Designing NPCs › Detailed NPCs › Mannerism — One behavior that makes the NPC easy for players to remember. _[npcs, description-narration]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › Designing NPCs › Detailed NPCs › Interactions with Others — One social trait describing how the NPC treats different people. _[npcs, social-interaction]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › Designing NPCs › Detailed NPCs › Useful Knowledge — One piece of information the NPC holds that can help or mislead the party. _[npcs, secrets-information]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › Designing NPCs › Detailed NPCs › Ideal — One guiding principle; players who learn it can use it as leverage. _[npcs, social-interaction]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › Designing NPCs › Detailed NPCs › Bond — The people, places, or things that matter most to the NPC. _[npcs]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › Designing NPCs › Detailed NPCs › Flaw or Secret — A weakness or hidden fact that can undermine the NPC and generate plot. _[npcs, secrets-information]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › Designing NPCs › Monsters as NPCs — Says significant named monsters deserve the same personality work as humanoid NPCs. _[npcs]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › Designing NPCs › NPC Statistics — Offers three ways to stat an NPC: minimal stats, a monster stat block, or class levels. _[npcs]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › NPC Party Members — Covers why NPCs join the party and how to keep them from stealing the spotlight. _[npcs, spotlight]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › NPC Party Members › Low-Level Followers — Covers handling followers such as squires or apprentices. _[npcs]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › NPC Party Members › Adventurer NPCs — Covers filling out a short-handed party with NPCs built to the party's level. _[npcs, players-and-expectations]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › NPC Party Members › Optional Rule: Loyalty — Optional secret loyalty score that governs how far an NPC companion will go for the party. _[npcs, secrets-information, house-rules]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › Contacts — Describes NPCs with ties to characters who supply information, supplies, and hooks without adventuring. _[npcs, adventure-design]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › Contacts › Patrons — Describes patrons who employ the party and supply quests and rewards. _[npcs, adventure-design]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › Hirelings — Short note that hirelings rarely join dangerous endeavors and where their rules live. _[npcs]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › Extras — Background creatures that can be promoted to larger roles when the story needs it. _[npcs, improvisation]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › Villains — Introduces tools for designing a villain's scheme, methods, and weakness. _[npcs, adventure-design]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › Villains › Villain's Scheme — Table-driven choices for what a villain wants (immortality, power, and so on). _[npcs, adventure-design]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › Villains › Villain's Methods — Table-driven choices for how the villain pursues the scheme. _[npcs, adventure-design]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › Villains › Villain's Secret Weakness — Teaches giving a villain a hidden weakness that players can discover and exploit. _[npcs, secrets-information, adventure-design]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 Creating Nonplayer Characters › Villainous Class Options — Heading-level only. Two villain-oriented subclass options (death cleric, oathbreaker paladin) for NPC antagonists. _[npcs]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 5 Adventure Environments › Urban Encounters — Covers law and order, trials, sentences, and random urban events. _[social-interaction, encounter-design]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 6 Between Adventures › Downtime Activities › More Downtime Activities — Covers building a stronghold, carousing, crafting magic items, gaining renown, sacred rites, running a business, selling items, sowing rumors, and training. _[time-tracking, rewards, social-interaction]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 8 Running the Game › Social Interaction — Frames social scenes as goal-driven and balances roleplay with checks. _[social-interaction]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 8 Running the Game › Social Interaction › Resolving Interactions — Four-step structure: starting attitude, conversation, Charisma check, optional repeat. _[social-interaction, adjudication]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 8 Running the Game › Social Interaction › Resolving Interactions › 1. Starting Attitude — Choose friendly, indifferent, or hostile as the baseline. _[social-interaction, npcs]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 8 Running the Game › Social Interaction › Resolving Interactions › 2. Conversation — Play out the dialogue; good arguments can shift attitude. _[social-interaction, player-agency]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 8 Running the Game › Social Interaction › Resolving Interactions › 3. Charisma Check — Call for a check when the request lands, with a table of DCs by attitude. _[social-interaction, ability-checks, dc-setting]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 8 Running the Game › Social Interaction › Resolving Interactions › 4. Repeat? — Warns that further pushing may backfire. _[social-interaction]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 8 Running the Game › Social Interaction › Roleplaying — Reassures DMs that portrayal need not be perfect and offers techniques. _[npcs, description-narration]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 8 Running the Game › Social Interaction › Roleplaying › Being the NPC — Voice an NPC through its ideals, bonds, and flaws. _[npcs]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 8 Running the Game › Social Interaction › Roleplaying › Using Your Voice — Vary volume and tone for effect and distinguish characters by voice. _[npcs, description-narration]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 8 Running the Game › Social Interaction › Roleplaying › Using Your Face and Arms — Use expression and gesture to convey emotion. _[npcs]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 8 Running the Game › Experience Points › Noncombat Challenges — Award XP for negotiations and other non-fight achievements at DM discretion. _[rewards, social-interaction]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 9 Dungeon Master's Workshop › Combat Options › Morale — Option for deciding when monsters flee. _[combat-running, npcs, improvisation]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 9 Dungeon Master's Workshop › Creating a Monster › NPC Stat Blocks — Customizing NPC archetypes and building from scratch. _[npcs]_
- DMG 2014 › Ch 9 Dungeon Master's Workshop › Creating a Monster › Monsters with Classes — Giving class levels to monsters. _[npcs, encounter-design]_

### Xanathar's Guide to Everything (XGtE, 2017)

- XGtE › Dungeon Master's Tools › Encounter Building › Step 5: Add Flavor — Teaches layering personality, relationships, terrain, traps, and off-screen events so a fight is more than numbers. _[encounter-design, npcs, description-narration]_
- XGtE › Dungeon Master's Tools › Random Encounters › Flight, or Fight, or ... ? — Reminds the DM that results can be social, avoided, or discovered and offers hooks for non-combat reactions. _[encounter-design, social-interaction, improvisation]_
- XGtE › Downtime Revisited › Rivals — Teaches introducing opposed NPCs who act during downtime and create complications. _[npcs, adventure-design]_
- XGtE › Downtime Revisited › Rivals › Creating a Rival — Build a rival with an NPC profile and a schedule of plans. _[npcs, adventure-design]_

### Tasha's Cauldron of Everything (TCoE, 2020)

- TCoE › Ch 4 Dungeon Master's Tools › Sidekicks — Provides a simple way to turn a low-level creature into a companion NPC that scales with the party. _[npcs, multiple-players]_
- TCoE › Ch 4 Dungeon Master's Tools › Sidekicks › Creating a Sidekick — Pick a creature of low challenge rating and layer a sidekick class on it. _[npcs]_
- TCoE › Ch 4 Dungeon Master's Tools › Sidekicks › Gaining a Sidekick Class — Choose Expert, Spellcaster, or Warrior; sidekicks track the party's average level. _[npcs]_
- TCoE › Ch 4 Dungeon Master's Tools › Sidekicks › Expert / Spellcaster / Warrior — Heading-level only. Class tables and features for the three sidekick classes. _[npcs]_
- TCoE › Ch 4 Dungeon Master's Tools › Parleying with Monsters — Teaches resolving a monster meeting through negotiation by offering what the creature wants. _[social-interaction, npcs, encounter-design]_
- TCoE › Ch 4 Dungeon Master's Tools › Parleying with Monsters › Monster Research — Characters can learn what a creature wants via skill checks by monster type, with a DC rule. _[social-interaction, ability-checks, secrets-information]_
- TCoE › Ch 4 Dungeon Master's Tools › Parleying with Monsters › Monsters' Desires — Per-monster-type notes on typical wants, to guide NPC motivation. _[npcs, social-interaction]_
