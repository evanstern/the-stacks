# Attitudes as Persistent State

_How sources define an NPC's attitude toward the party, whether it lasts, and what changes it. Neutral and descriptive._

## SRD 5.2.1 (CC-BY-4.0)

- An NPC's attitude toward a character is Friendly, Indifferent or Hostile; Friendly NPCs are predisposed to help and Hostile ones inclined to hinder (SRD p.10, Social Interaction).
- Friendly: Advantage on an ability check to influence it (SRD p.182, Glossary: Friendly). Hostile: Disadvantage on the check (SRD p.183, Glossary: Hostile). Indifferent: no desire to help or hinder, and "the default attitude of a monster" (SRD p.184, Glossary: Indifferent; SRD 5.2.1, CC-BY-4.0).
- The Influence action ends with a waiting rule: after a failed check the character must wait 24 hours, or a duration the GM sets, before urging the monster the same way again (SRD p.184, Glossary: Influence). The SRD text read does not say how an attitude changes or how long it lasts.
- The Charmed condition gives the charmer Advantage on any ability check to interact with the charmed creature socially (SRD p.178). The condition is a temporary state with its own duration.
- The SRD says a character can learn an NPC's goals from the GM's portrayal and then use them to influence it (SRD p.11, Social Interaction).

## Books

- DMG 2014 › Ch 8 › Social Interaction › Resolving Interactions › 1. Starting Attitude: the DM picks friendly, indifferent or hostile as the baseline for the interaction. A hostile creature may be so ill-disposed that no check helps and diplomacy fails automatically.
- DMG 2014 › Ch 8 › Social Interaction › Resolving Interactions › 2. Conversation › Changing Attitude: attitude can shift during a conversation. Touching a creature's ideal, bond or flaw can move a hostile creature to temporarily indifferent, or an indifferent one to temporarily friendly. A gaffe, insult or harmful deed moves it the other way. The usual limit is one step per interaction, temporary or permanent. The DM decides whether the party framed its words in terms that matter to the creature.
- DMG 2014 › Ch 8 › Social Interaction › Resolving Interactions › 4. Repeat?: pushing again after a check can make the subject hostile.
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 › NPC Party Members › Optional Rule: Loyalty: a secret numeric score from 0 to 20 for a companion NPC. Its maximum equals the highest Charisma score in the party and it starts at half that. Help with the NPC's bond or good treatment raises it by 1d4; acts against its alignment or bond lower it by 1d4, and selfish abuse lowers it by 2d4. At 0 the NPC stops serving the party's interests; at 10 or more it risks life and limb. The DM keeps the score in secret. The rule is optional.
- DMG 2014 › Ch 3 › Adventure Types › Intrigue: tracks the party's influence with factions or individuals as the adventure's structure; the book frames favors and standing as the currency.
- XGtE › Downtime Revisited › Rivals: a rival's agenda changes over time because rivals keep acting between adventures.
- VRGtR › Horror Adventures › Running Horror Games › Horror Pacing › Reliability and Trust: some allies and places should be reliably trustworthy, because frequent betrayals encourage paranoia.

## Web

- Random D&D Tip: Reactions & Relationships (https://thealexandrian.net/?p=50021): the DM assigns an attitude from close and friendly to hostile, from either a long-term relationship or an immediate reaction. On a first meeting with no history the DM may roll 2d6 on a reaction table. Persistent attitudes (an old friend, a rival) bias future meetings; situational attitudes are scene-bound and usually win during that scene. Relationships shift gradually across sessions, while first impressions can change faster.
- Resolving Social Actions (https://theangrygm.com/resolving-social-actions/): the NPC carries a five-element social stat block. Disposition runs on about five levels, is set by first impressions, and moves with successful Connect actions or emotionally charged interactions such as bullying. Patience only drains, roughly one point per social action, and at moderate difficulty lasts about six actions. At zero the NPC stonewalls and further social actions fail without a roll. Mood is a temporary emotional state used as a situational modifier.
- Resolving Social Interactions: Social Conflict Not Really Defined (https://theangrygm.com/social-conflict-not-really-defined/): an NPC's state mixes mood, default temperament, fears and anger, rapport and trust. The article says traits are not settled until they are revealed at the table.
- Advanced NPC Roleplaying Templates (https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/46250/roleplaying-games/advanced-npc-templates): one page per NPC with room on the back to note updates for later sessions. A commenter suggests a Goals section. An experimental Social Profile maps tactics that help or hurt to advantage or disadvantage.
- Reaction and reputation search summary (https://thealexandrian.net/?p=50021 plus search leads named in [[_grounding]]): one house rule replaces a reputation score with a table of past deeds; another adds a renown roll. The search summary reports the difficulty of per-NPC tags and the argument that a GM-kept note is simpler. These are search-lead claims, not read in full.

## Where sources disagree

- Duration and size of change: DMG 2014 limits an interaction to one step and calls the shift temporary or permanent at the DM's call. The Alexandrian says relationships move over many sessions and first impressions can move faster. The Angry GM models Disposition as moving with individual actions and Patience as a depletable counter. The SRD text read states neither a limit nor a duration beyond the 24-hour retry wait.
- Number of levels: three (SRD 5.2.1, DMG 2014), four bands on the Alexandrian's reaction table per the search summary, about five (Angry GM).
- Edition divergence: DMG 2014 sets DC by attitude with a table; SRD 5.2.1 gives Advantage or Disadvantage by attitude and a flat default DC. See [[Social-Checks-and-DCs]].
