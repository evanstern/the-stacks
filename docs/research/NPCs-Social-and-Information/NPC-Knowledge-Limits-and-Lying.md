# NPC Knowledge Limits and Lying

_What sources say an NPC knows, withholds or lies about, and how players are meant to detect it. Neutral and descriptive. This area is thin in the sources; gaps are named._

## SRD 5.2.1 (CC-BY-4.0)

- Zone of Truth: creatures that fail a Charisma save cannot speak a deliberate lie in the zone, but may stay evasive and must be truthful (SRD p.175, spell: Zone of Truth). The affected creature is aware of the spell.
- Detect Thoughts: Read Thoughts learns what is most on a target's mind; a target that knows no language and is not telepathic yields nothing (SRD p.123, spell: Detect Thoughts). Sense Thoughts detects only the presence of thinking creatures.
- Search action with Insight: discern a creature's state of mind (SRD p.187). Study action: call to mind knowledge, with the Areas of Knowledge table naming which skill covers which subject (SRD p.189).
- The Influence action marks a request "unwilling" when it is repugnant to the monster, in which case the monster does not comply without a check (SRD p.184). The SRD text read has no rule for NPC lying, for limits on what an NPC may know, or for a check to detect a lie.

## Books

- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 › Designing NPCs › Detailed NPCs › Useful Knowledge: one sentence naming a piece of knowledge that may be banal (the best inn) or key (a clue to a murder). The book frames knowledge as one authored field per NPC.
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 › Detailed NPCs › Flaw or Secret: a weakness or a secret the NPC tries to hide. Characters who uncover it can use it to influence the NPC.
- DMG 2014 › Ch 3 › Adventure Types › Mysteries › Suspects: more than one suspect should hold a secret, so an innocent suspect may seem nervous or try to lie.
- DMG 2014 › Ch 3 › Mysteries › Clues: verbal clues come from suspects and witnesses; some clues point at the wrong suspect.
- DMG 2014 › Ch 8 › Social Interaction › Determining Characteristics: a failed Insight check by 10 or more may deliver a false characteristic (the example inverts a sage's flaw). The book thereby treats a misleading result as a possible outcome of a failed read.
- TCoE › Ch 4 › Parleying with Monsters › Monster Research: characters learn what a creature wants through skill checks by type.
- VRGtR › Horror Adventures › Running Horror Games › Horror Pacing › Undermine Reality (listed in the DM-Craft-Map branch, title level only): blurs what is real; not read for this branch.

## Web

- Player Trust: A Definitive Guide (https://pooltime.bearblog.dev/player-trust-definitive-guide/): the GM must portray the world truthfully, but that duty does not automatically cover what NPCs say; NPCs may be mistaken or deceptive. The author prefers logic over Sense Motive rolls: behavior that conflicts with motives the players already know should reveal the lie. An unknown motive should show signs of concealment. A false stated motive should fall apart under scrutiny. Two mutually exclusive stated motives can hint at deception. Players should understand what they risk by trusting the NPC. Elaborate cons leak truth. The author advises defining and foreshadowing motives in advance so lies are not unfair.
- GM advice search summary (leads named in [[_grounding]]): several GMs treat a failed insight check as no meaningful evidence rather than a false reading; a Pathfinder rules excerpt suggests describing body language or odd word choices rather than declaring a lie; one forum thread distinguishes an NPC who lies from one who believes a falsehood (an erroneous truth). These are search-result claims from forum, rules-site and blog pages, not read in full.
- Help! My Players are Talking to Things! (https://theangrygm.com/help-my-players-are-talking-to-things/): do not hide an NPC's price or motives as a puzzle; give clues about what would move the NPC; an NPC who wants something will eventually say so.
- Roleplaying for the Beginning GM (https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/52707/roleplaying-games/roleplaying-for-the-beginning-gm): an NPC may hold information it is reluctant to share; play the reluctance but do not use it to shut the PCs out (Default to Yes, Failing Forward keep the scene moving).
- Declaring Your Players' Social Actions (https://theangrygm.com/declaring-social-actions/): most NPCs will volunteer information if allowed to talk. The article does not address NPC lies.
- Resolving Social Actions (https://theangrygm.com/resolving-social-actions/): the article does not address NPC lies directly; it advises portraying each stat block element at least once so a perceptive player can work out why the NPC behaves as it does.
- Running Mysteries: Returning to the Scene of the Crime (https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/49595/roleplaying-games/running-mysteries-returning-to-the-scene-of-the-crime): a witness questioned early may know more later; GUMSHOE-style leveraged clues unlock new information from an NPC once the PCs hold a prerequisite clue.
- Advanced NPC Roleplaying Templates (https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/46250/roleplaying-games/advanced-npc-templates): Key Info holds what the NPC knows that matters to the scenario; Key Info – Reactions holds information released only when PCs prompt it the right way.

## Where sources disagree

- Detection: Pathfinder-style rules excerpts and DMG 2014 use a skill check (with the DMG allowing a false result on a bad fail); the Player Trust article prefers logic from known motives over a roll; a forum summary reads failure as no evidence.
- Whether an NPC may lie to the players at all: the Player Trust article says the world-truth duty stops at the NPC's mouth; the Mysteries section of DMG 2014 expects innocent suspects to lie.
- No gathered source gives a rule that limits an NPC's knowledge to what it could have witnessed. Knowledge is treated as an authored list.
