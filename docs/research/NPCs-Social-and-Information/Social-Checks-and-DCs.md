# Social Checks and DCs

_When sources call for a social roll, which check they name, how the DC is set, and what failure and retries do. Neutral and descriptive._

## SRD 5.2.1 (CC-BY-4.0)

- Social interactions progress in two ways, through roleplaying and ability checks (SRD p.10, Social Interaction). Ability checks can decide an outcome when the GM wants dice to play a role, and the GM will typically ask for the Influence action (SRD p.11).
- Influence action: the player describes or roleplays how the character communicates. The GM then decides whether the monster is willing, unwilling or hesitant (SRD p.184, Glossary: Influence).
  - Willing: the urging aligns with the monster's desires; no check; the monster fulfils the request in a way it prefers.
  - Unwilling: repugnant to the monster or counter to its alignment; no check; it does not comply.
  - Hesitant: an ability check is needed, affected by attitude (Advantage Friendly, Disadvantage Hostile).
- The GM chooses the check. The default DC is 15 or the monster's Intelligence score, whichever is higher. On success the monster does as urged. On failure the character waits 24 hours, or a GM-set duration, before urging it the same way again (SRD p.184).
- The Influence Checks table: Charisma (Deception) to deceive a monster that understands you; Charisma (Intimidation); Charisma (Performance) to amuse; Charisma (Persuasion) for a monster that understands you; Wisdom (Animal Handling) to gently coax a Beast or Monstrosity (SRD p.184).
- Search action with Insight reads a creature's state of mind (SRD p.187, Glossary: Search table). The rules text notes a character can Influence or Search to read body language but not both at once (SRD p.10, One Thing at a Time).
- Edition divergence: the SRD 5.2.1 text read applies the Influence action to monsters and does not print a DC-by-attitude table; DMG 2014 prints one (below).

## Books

- DMG 2014 › Ch 8 › Social Interaction › Resolving Interactions › 3. Charisma Check: call for the check when the party reaches its request, demand or suggestion, or when the conversation has run its course. Any participant may roll. Persuasion, Deception or Intimidation may apply. The creature's attitude and the size of the risk or sacrifice asked set the DC through a Conversation Reaction table keyed to friendly, indifferent and hostile (DCs 0, 10 and 20). At DC 20 a friendly creature accepts a significant risk or sacrifice; an indifferent one a minor risk or sacrifice; a hostile one acts only when no risk is involved.
- DMG 2014 › same section › Aiding the Check and Multiple Checks: helpers who contribute meaningfully give the roller advantage; a counterproductive remark gives disadvantage; more than one check may be called when the party has several goals.
- DMG 2014 › Ch 8 › Social Interaction (opening): some DMs run social scenes as free-form roleplay with few dice; others call Charisma checks; most tables mix player skill and character skill.
- DMG 2014 › Ch 8 › Social Interaction › Determining Characteristics: a Wisdom (Insight) check, with a DM-set DC, can uncover one of a creature's characteristics; failing by 10 or more may produce a false or inverted characteristic.
- TCoE › Ch 4 › Parleying with Monsters: offering something the creature wants can grant advantage on communication checks; Monster Research uses a skill chosen by creature type with DC 10 plus the creature's Challenge Rating.
- DMG 2014 › Ch 8 › Experience Points › Noncombat Challenges: XP for negotiation at DM discretion.

## Web

- Resolving Social Actions (https://theangrygm.com/resolving-social-actions/): the DC starts from Disposition, then adjusts by action type (Convince hardest, then Negotiate, Understand, Connect easiest), by how the approach fits Mood and Motivations, and by the NPC's skills and role. Before rolling, the GM decides what success and failure would do (win, loss, progress, setback, buff, debuff). The third repeat of the same point can be resolved as an action. At zero Patience, actions fail without a roll.
- Declaring Your Players' Social Actions (https://theangrygm.com/declaring-social-actions/): the GM turns each declaration into an intention and an approach; the player's words do not decide the result alone; dice are not fudged.
- Help! My Players are Talking to Things! (https://theangrygm.com/help-my-players-are-talking-to-things/): roll only when a request can succeed or fail and the outcome matters; an NPC with no reason to refuse grants the request without dice; the check asks whether the player's approach can overcome an Objection or appeal to an Incentive. The article discourages bonuses for acting ability, because acting is a talent not all players share.
- Random D&D Tip: Reactions & Relationships (https://thealexandrian.net/?p=50021): the DM decides whether a check is needed at all (none when success is certain or impossible), and controls retries without rerolls.
- Running Roleplay and Intrigue Adventures (https://slyflourish.com/running_intrigue_adventures.html): call for a roll only when failure is a real possibility that would not stall the adventure; treat the result as a matter of degree; a strong player's approach can outweigh the character's stats, and a gifted character with a less skilled player can lean on the character's ability.
- The Art of Rulings family (see the Resolution and Outcome branch for the read of that series; not repeated here).

## Where sources disagree

- Who the roll measures: SRD 5.2.1 and DMG 2014 treat a Charisma-based roll as the character's skill. Sly Flourish lets a strong player's approach count for more. The Angry GM says words do not decide the result alone and discourages acting-based bonuses.
- Whether a roll is needed: SRD 5.2.1 skips the check for willing or unwilling requests; DMG 2014 calls for a check when the request lands; the Angry GM says no roll when the NPC has no objection.
- Retry: SRD 5.2.1 uses a 24-hour wait; DMG 2014 warns repeated pushing can anger; the Angry GM drains Patience.
