# NPC Records and Motives

_What fields sources give an NPC, which are shown to players and which are hidden, and how motives are written. Neutral and descriptive._

## SRD 5.2.1 (CC-BY-4.0)

- The SRD's NPC material is the stat block. The social text says a character can learn an NPC's goals from the GM's portrayal (SRD p.11). The SRD text read defines no NPC personality record.

## Books

- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 › Designing NPCs › Quick NPCs: one or two memorable traits; combat statistics only when the NPC poses a threat.
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 › Detailed NPCs: ten one-sentence elements: occupation and history, appearance, abilities, talent, mannerism, interactions with others, useful knowledge, ideal, bond, and flaw or secret.
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 › Detailed NPCs › Ideal, Bond, Flaw or Secret: characters who uncover an ideal, a bond or a flaw can use it to influence the NPC (cross-reference to Ch 8 social rules).
- DMG 2014 › Ch 8 › Social Interaction › Roleplaying › Being the NPC: portray the NPC through ideals, flaws and bonds; show rather than tell; twists in response make the world feel alive.
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 › Villains (Scheme, Methods, Secret Weakness): a villain gets a scheme, methods, and a hidden weakness the players can find and exploit.
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 › NPC Party Members: companions are run by the DM or handed to a player, and the DM keeps them portrayed as characters. The loyalty score is kept secret. See [[Attitudes-as-Persistent-State]].
- DMG 2014 › Ch 4 › Contacts: a recurring contact deserves goals and a thought for how those goals come into play.
- DMG 2014 › Ch 3 › Event-Based Adventures: the villain's timeline of actions and the villain's reactions to interference form the structure.
- XGtE › Downtime Revisited › Rivals › Creating a Rival: a rival is built as an NPC with a schedule of plans; the best rivals link to the characters' backstories and recent events.
- VRGtR › Creating Domains of Dread › Creating a Darklord: design the villain first; give a past life, ideals, bonds and flaws, and make the villain culpable through choices.
- TCoE › Ch 4 › Parleying with Monsters › Monsters' Desires: per-type notes on what a creature wants.

## Web

- Universal NPC Roleplaying Template (https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/37916/roleplaying-games/universal-npc-roleplaying-template): fields: name, appearance, an optional quote, roleplaying bullets (one physical mannerism, personality, attitude, habits), background kept only if it affects decisions, key info, optional stat block. No separate motivation field. Brief content is the stated goal.
- Advanced NPC Roleplaying Templates (https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/46250/roleplaying-games/advanced-npc-templates): Key Info – Actions (proactive social moves), Key Info – Reactions (info released when prompted), Key Info – Resources (what the NPC can offer).
- Roleplaying for the Beginning GM (https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/52707/roleplaying-games/roleplaying-for-the-beginning-gm): give each NPC an objective stated in one first-person sentence that often runs counter to what the PCs want; add a touchstone mannerism.
- Resolving Social Actions (https://theangrygm.com/resolving-social-actions/): Source of Conflict, Disposition, Patience, Motivation, Mood. Each Motivation cuts both ways, as a lever or a trap.
- Help! My Players are Talking to Things! (https://theangrygm.com/help-my-players-are-talking-to-things/): an NPC has an Objection and may have an Incentive; state them so players are not guessing.
- Create Villains (https://slyflourish.com/creating_villains.html): a motive that is easy to understand, three plans toward the goal so no one plan is the only route, villains stay busy off-screen; the article does not address villain secrets.
- Running Roleplay and Intrigue (https://slyflourish.com/running_intrigue_adventures.html): prepare each NPC's motives, attitude and knowledge; note what they know, what they want and which secrets or clues they might reveal.
- Don't Prep Plots: The Principles of RPG Villainy (https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/36383/roleplaying-games/dont-prep-plots-you-will-rue-this-day-heroes-the-principles-of-rpg-villainy): prepare a pool of antagonists, their resources and off-screen activities; improvise which one survives and becomes the nemesis; most antagonists should flee when a fight turns.
- Player Trust (https://pooltime.bearblog.dev/player-trust-definitive-guide/): define and foreshadow NPC motives in advance.

## Where sources disagree

- Whether motive is a field: DMG 2014 stores it as ideal, bond and flaw; the Angry GM as Motivation plus Source of Conflict; the Alexandrian's template has no field and derives it from roleplaying bullets and background; Sly Flourish keeps motives, attitude and knowledge as prep notes.
- Whether a motive is hidden: DMG 2014 hides flaw or secret, and says finding an ideal or flaw helps players. The Angry GM says to portray every stat block element once so a perceptive player can work it out. The Player Trust article says to define and foreshadow motives so deceit is fair. Help! says do not hide a price as a puzzle.
