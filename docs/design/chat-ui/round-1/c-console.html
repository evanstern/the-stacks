> How does grappling work?

Grappling is a way to control a creature in melee. Instead of a normal weapon strike, you can use the Attack action to make a special melee attack, a grapple . A successful grapple doesn’t harm the target, but it does latch on and restrict movement, matching what’s described in the grappling rules .

While grappled, a creature’s speed drops to 0, and it can’t benefit from any bonus that would increase its speed .

A grappled creature can spend its action to escape a grapple; doing so requires a check against the grappler’s escape DC, and success ends the condition .

Basic Rules › Combat › Grappling

Grappling represents a contest of strength and leverage rather than a weapon strike. Instead of one of your attacks, you can use the Attack action to make a special melee attack, a grapple, provided you have a hand free. If the attack succeeds, the target is grappled: its speed becomes 0, and it can’t benefit from any bonus to speed until the grapple ends. A creature within 5 feet of you can attempt to grapple as normal, but ranged attacks cannot initiate a grapple. Some creatures are too large to be grappled by a creature smaller than them, unless the smaller creature has a trait that says otherwise. The grapple ends automatically if you are ever more than 5 feet from the grappled creature.

Basic Rules › Appendix: Conditions › Grappled

A grappled creature’s speed becomes 0, and it loses any bonus to its speed for as long as the condition lasts.

Basic Rules › Combat › Grappling › Escaping a Grapple

A grappled creature can use its action to escape a grapple, contesting its ability check against the grappler’s. Success frees the creature and ends the condition immediately.
